# Strategy Document Tone Guide

## Core voice

- Use UK English.
- Be precise, practical, and evidence-led.
- Write in clear declarative sentences.
- Use `we` for organisational recommendations.
- Keep confidence measured and tied to data.

## Token-efficient generation rules

- Start with a compact executive summary (150-300 words).
- Keep sections scoped to decisions, not background essays.
- Use tables for options, trade-offs, costs, and risks.
- Avoid repeating the same rationale in multiple sections.
- Include appendices for deep detail; keep core narrative tight.

## Inclusive language recommendations

- Avoid assumptions about user groups, teams, or markets.
- Use neutral terms for roles and stakeholders.
- Describe impacted groups respectfully and specifically.
- Avoid deficit framing; focus on barriers and design choices.
- Replace exclusionary terms with neutral alternatives.

## Document structure

1. Executive summary
2. Context and constraints
3. Options assessed
4. Recommendation and rationale
5. Cost, risk, and mitigation
6. Delivery plan and milestones
7. Success metrics

## Style details

- Use active voice for decisions and actions.
- Define terms and acronyms on first use.
- Quantify claims wherever possible.
- Separate observed facts from assumptions.
- State known unknowns explicitly.

## Phrases to use

- "Based on our analysis..."
- "The data indicates..."
- "Key risks and mitigations are..."
- "We recommend..."

## Phrases to avoid

- "Best-in-class", "world-class", "groundbreaking"
- "Leverage" (as filler verb)
- "Obviously", "clearly"
- "In the ever changing world of..."
