# Professional Email Tone Guide

Email is where the coffee-shop voice gets compressed. Same person, less room. Read
[the blog guide](../blog/AGENTS.md) first - this one only covers what changes.

## Core voice

- Use UK English.
- Be direct and specific. Say what you want in the first line.
- Use contractions. A stiff email reads as a cross email.
- Use first-person singular (`I`) for things I own, `we` for team decisions.
- Calm confidence, not apology. Skip "sorry to bother you" and "just wanted to".
- Understatement still works here. "That timing is going to be tight" lands harder than "this is a major concern".

## Length

Default to 80-180 words. One request per email where you can manage it. Paragraphs of
one to three sentences.

If it needs more than 180 words, it probably needs a document and a short email pointing
at it. Link or attach the detail instead of pasting it.

## Structure

1. Subject line stating the outcome and the timing.
2. Greeting matched to the relationship.
3. First line says why I'm writing.
4. Short context, then the decision or request.
5. The next step, with an owner and a date.
6. Sign-off.

## Style details

- Lead with the ask, then the reasoning. Not the other way round.
- Be explicit about deadlines. "By Tuesday 15:00", not "soon".
- Separate what I know from what I'm assuming.
- Quantify where you can.
- Don't bury a question in the middle of a paragraph.

## Inclusive language

The same rules as the blog, with a few that matter more in correspondence.

- Use gender-neutral language by default (`they`, `everyone`, `team`, `folks`).
- Use neutral forms of address unless someone has told you their preference. Match the name and pronouns they use.
- Don't assume role, seniority, gender, culture, working hours, or family structure. Someone's out-of-office isn't a story you need to fill in.
- Prefer globally clear wording over local idiom. Cricket and rounders metaphors don't travel.
- Keep requests non-diminishing. "Could you review this" rather than "just quickly do this".
- Describe requirements and behaviours, not personal traits.
- If wording might exclude someone, suggest a neutral rewrite.

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| Hi guys | Hi everyone, Hi team |
| manpower, man-hours | effort, staffing, person-hours |
| sanity check | quick check, sense check |
| Dear Sir/Madam | Dear [name], Hello |
| blacklist/whitelist | denylist/allowlist |
| grandfathered | legacy status |

Watch the timing assumptions too. "End of day" means different things across time zones,
and not everyone works Monday to Friday. Give an actual date and zone.

## Phrases to use

- "Could you please review this by Tuesday 15:00?"
- "The decision I need is..."
- "The risk of waiting is..."
- "If this timing doesn't work, tell me what does."
- "No action needed - this is for information."

## Phrases to avoid

- "Just checking in"
- "As per my previous email"
- "ASAP"
- "Sorry to bother you"
- "Hope this finds you well"
- "Obviously", "clearly", "simply", "it's easy to"
- "Please advise", "kindly", "be advised"
- "Circle back", "touch base", "leverage", "reach out"
