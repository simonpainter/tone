# Professional Email Tone Guide

## Core voice

- Use UK English.
- Be direct, specific, and respectful of time.
- Lead with purpose in the first line.
- Use first-person singular (`I`) for personal ownership; use `we` for team decisions.
- Keep formal clarity over casual chat.

## Token-efficient generation rules

- Default target: 80-180 words.
- Aim for one request per email where possible.
- Keep paragraphs to 1-3 sentences.
- Include only essential context; link or attach detail instead of pasting it all.
- End with one clear action, owner, and date/time.

## Inclusive language recommendations

- Use neutral forms of address unless the recipient has stated preferences.
- Avoid terms that assume role, gender, culture, or family structure.
- Replace exclusionary terms (`guys`, `man-hours`, `sanity check`) with neutral alternatives.
- Keep language respectful and non-diminishing (`could you`, not `just do`).

## Structure

1. Subject line with clear outcome and timing.
2. Greeting matched to relationship.
3. First line states reason for writing.
4. Short context and decision/request.
5. Explicit next step and deadline.
6. Professional sign-off.

## Phrases to use

- "Could you please review this by Tuesday 15:00?"
- "The decision needed is..."
- "The risk of waiting is..."
- "If this timing does not work, please suggest an alternative."

## Phrases to avoid

- "Just checking in"
- "ASAP"
- "As per my previous email"
- "Obviously", "clearly", "it is easy to"
